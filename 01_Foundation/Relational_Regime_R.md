# Relational Regime R

## 1. Purpose

The Relational Regime \(R\) is the network of admissible geometric and topological relations among identity boundaries established within the bounded substrate \(S\).

It defines the first stable relational organization that becomes available after identity has been stabilized and bounded substrate has emerged.

---

## 2. Scope Boundary

This node defines:

- the relational network \(R\);
- the emergence of relations from bounded substrate \(S\);
- the admissible organization of identity boundaries;
- the transition from static substrate to structured relational behavior.

This node does not define:

- final dynamical laws;
- effective Lagrangian structure;
- explicit geometric field equations.

Those belong to downstream formalism and dynamical nodes.

---

## 3. Core Relation

The relational regime emerges from the bounded substrate through admissibility:

\[
S \Rightarrow_A R
\]

Here, \(A\) denotes admissibility.  
This means that \(R\) is not arbitrary relation, but relation filtered and stabilized by admissibility conditions acting over \(S\).

---

## 4. Definition

\(R\) is the set of admissible relations that connect identity boundaries within \(S\).

It may be viewed as the structured relational organization that converts bounded admissible configurations into a network of persistent relations.

\[
R = \{ \text{all admissible relations in } S \}
\]

---

## 5. Functional Roles

The relational regime performs the following functions:

### 5.1 Relation formation
It provides a formal basis for the formation of relations among admissible identities.

### 5.2 Boundary organization
It organizes identity boundaries into a coherent relational network.

### 5.3 Transition support
It supports the transition from bounded substrate to later geometric, topological, and dynamical structures.

---

## 6. Structural Properties

The regime \(R\) is characterized by:

- **admissibility dependence**: all relations must pass admissibility constraints;
- **boundary sensitivity**: relations are defined with respect to identity boundaries;
- **network coherence**: relations are not isolated but form a connected regime;
- **substrate dependence**: \(R\) cannot exist independently of \(S\).

---

## 7. Relation to Upstream Nodes

\(R\) depends on:

- `Identity_Constraint_Cid` for stable identity;
- `Bounded_Substrate_S` for the admissible domain;
- `Admissibility_Principle_AA` for relational validation.

Without these upstream conditions, relational structure does not stabilize into a valid regime.

---

## 8. Relation to Downstream Nodes

The relational regime is upstream of:

- `Emergent_Law_L`
- `Formalism_F`

This means that law-like structure and explicit formalism are built on top of \(R\), not before it.
